% MOTIVATION + CONTRIBUTIONS   %
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\selectfontsize[baselinefaktor=13/10]{26pt}

\begin{textfeld}{6cm}{72cm}%
\cdblock[width=72.1cm, columnnum=2, columnspace=3cm, outertop=verylong, outerbottom=long, inner=long]{\selectfontsize{36pt}\underline{\textbf{Motivation \& Contributions}}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Why Urdu?}}\\[\baselineskip]%
Large Language Models are evaluated mostly on English. For low-resource, morphologically rich, head-final languages such as \textbf{Urdu} (Indo-Aryan, $\sim$230M speakers, Perso-Arabic script), it is unclear which grammatical phenomena multilingual LLMs actually learn.\\[0.4\baselineskip]%
Existing minimal-pair benchmarks (BLiMP, CLiMP, SLING, RuBLiMP, TurBLiMP, BLiMP-NL, MultiBLiMP) do not provide broad coverage for Urdu syntax.}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Our contributions}}\\[\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~A new \textbf{minimal-pairs benchmark for Urdu} built from naturally occurring text (Urdu Treebank + in-house corpus).\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~Coverage of \textbf{typologically distinctive} phenomena: split ergativity, dative experiencers, honorific agreement, obliqueness, participial relatives, word-order variation.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Two rounds of human validation} with 17 native speakers (1{,}077 pairs).\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~Evaluation of \textbf{20+ LLMs} (BERT, mT5, BLOOMZ, Granite, Gemma-3, LLaMA-3, DeepSeek-R1, Alif-Urdu, GPT-4o).}%
\end{textfeld}%

\begin{textfeld}{6cm}{125cm}%
\cdblock[width=72.1cm, columnnum=2, columnspace=3cm, outertop=verylong, outerbottom=long, inner=long]{\selectfontsize{36pt}\underline{\textbf{Linguistic Phenomena in UrBLiMP}}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Universal \& core syntactic}}\\[0.5\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Subject--Verb Agreement} (gender, number, person)\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Noun--Adjective Agreement}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Dative Object} marking (\textit{ko})\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Word-Order Variation} in relative clauses\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Participial Relatives}\\[0.5\baselineskip]%
\textit{Example (gender agr., long distance):}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
\foreignlanguage{arabic}{استانی سبق پڑھا کر آرام سے کرسی پر بیٹھ کر سوتی۔}\\
\textit{`After teaching, the teacher would sit and sleep.'}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Typologically distinctive (Indo-Aryan)}}\\[0.5\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Aspect Agreement} (\textit{-ta:} / \textit{raha:} / \textit{cuka:})\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Ergativity} -- split system, \textit{ne} marking\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Experiencer Subject} -- dative \textit{mujhe}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Honorific} plural agreement\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Obliqueness} (adj., noun, pronoun, plural, verb)\\[0.5\baselineskip]%
\textit{Minimal pair (Ergativity):}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
\textbf{\textcolor{seeblau100}{$\checkmark$}}~\foreignlanguage{arabic}{پہرے‌دار نے تمام ماجرا راجا کو سنایا}\\[0.1\baselineskip]
\textbf{\textcolor{schwarz60}{$\times$}}~\foreignlanguage{arabic}{پہرے‌دار نے تمام ماجرا راجا کو سناتا}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
\textit{(ergative \textit{ne} requires perfective verb form)}}%
\end{textfeld}%

% METHOD / DATA CONSTRUCTION   %
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\selectfontsize[baselinefaktor=13/10]{26pt}

\begin{textfeld}{6cm}{183cm}%
\cdblock[width=72.1cm, columnnum=3, columnspace=3cm, outertop=verylong, outerbottom=long, inner=long]{\selectfontsize{36pt}\underline{\textbf{Method}}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{1. Source corpora}}\\[\baselineskip]%
\textbf{Urdu Treebank} (CLE, 7{,}854 sentences, Penn-Treebank style) -- extract sentences via syntactic patterns, e.g.\ \texttt{(VC (VBF) (AUXP))} for aspect.\\[0.4\baselineskip]%
\textbf{In-house Urdu corpus} -- regex-based extraction (e.g.\ \foreignlanguage{arabic}{جو\dots وہ\dots}) for paradigms with too few treebank hits.}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{2. Pair generation}}\\[\baselineskip]%
Rule-based perturbation of one minimal feature (\textit{e.g.}\ swap aspectual auxiliary, drop \textit{ko}, change verb gender) to derive the \textbf{ungrammatical} counterpart of each naturally occurring sentence.\\[0.4\baselineskip]%
Manual review + typo cleaning per paradigm.}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{3. Human validation (PCIbex)}}\\[\baselineskip]%
\textbf{17 native Urdu speakers}, two rounds.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
Round 1: 20-pair training set.\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
Round 2: $\sim$190 pairs per annotator, 10 per paradigm, randomised, forced-choice acceptability.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
Validated subset: \textbf{1{,}077 pairs}.}%
\end{textfeld}%

\selectfontsize[baselinefaktor=13/10]{26pt}

\begin{textfeld}{6cm}{226cm}%
\cdblock[width=72.1cm, columnnum=2, columnspace=3cm, outertop=verylong, outerbottom=long, inner=long]{\selectfontsize{36pt}\underline{\textbf{Evaluation Setup}}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Models (20+)}}\\[\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Encoder:} mBERT\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Encoder--decoder:} mT5-small, mT5-large\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Decoder:} Gemma-3 (1B/4B/12B/27B, pt+it), LLaMA-3-8B/70B, BLOOMZ-7B, Granite-3.3 (2B/8B, base+inst), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-70B\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Urdu-tuned:} Alif-1.0-8B-Instruct\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Closed:} GPT-4o (Base, Grammar-Prompt, GP+CoT)}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Metric}}\\[\baselineskip]%
Sentence-level \textbf{accuracy}: fraction of pairs where the model assigns lower (pseudo-)perplexity to the grammatical sentence.\\[0.4\baselineskip]%
\[\text{Acc} = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}\!\left[\text{ppl}(s^{i}_{\text{good}}) < \text{ppl}(s^{i}_{\text{bad}})\right]\]\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
GPT-4o evaluated via two-choice prompting (\textit{explain-then-process}).}%
\end{textfeld}%

% FINDINGS / ANALYSIS          %
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\selectfontsize[baselinefaktor=13/10]{26pt}

\begin{textfeld}{6cm}{322cm}%
\cdblock[width=72.1cm, columnnum=3, columnspace=3cm, outertop=verylong, outerbottom=long, inner=long]{\selectfontsize{36pt}\underline{\textbf{Key Findings}}}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Long-distance agreement is hard}}\\[\baselineskip]%
Even LLaMA-3-70B fails when subject and verb are separated by intervening clauses, e.g.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
\foreignlanguage{arabic}{استانی سبق پڑھا کر \dots سوتی}\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
The longer the dependency, the more LMs default to local cues.}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Size $\neq$ better}}\\[\baselineskip]%
Within Gemma-3, 1B$\to$27B yields significant gains (Wilcoxon, $p<0.05$).\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
\textbf{But} Gemma-3-27B-pt rivals LLaMA-3-70B / DeepSeek-70B -- \emph{no} significant difference among top models.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
\textbf{Pretrained $>$ Instruction-tuned} on Gemma-3 (1B/4B/12B): instruction tuning can \emph{hurt} grammatical sensitivity.}%
{\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{Phenomenon-specific}}\\[\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Aspect, Dative}: near-perfect (local cues).\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Obliqueness}: hard for adjectives \& masc.\ sg.\ nouns (subtle morphology).\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Subj--Verb gender}: hardest, max 92.5\%.\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Honorific}: requires world / register knowledge.\\[0.2\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~UrBLiMP-SVA is \emph{harder} than MultiBLiMP-SVA for the same models.}%
\end{textfeld}%

\textcolor{seeblau100}{\textbf{\selectfontsize{34pt}Take-aways}}\\[0.6cm]%
$\bullet$~UrBLiMP is the first broad-coverage minimal-pair benchmark for \textbf{Urdu syntax}.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~Multilingual LLMs handle \textbf{local agreement} well, but struggle with \textbf{long-distance dependencies} and \textbf{morpho-syntactic case}.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
$\bullet$~\textbf{Grammar prompting} brings GPT-4o above human average.\\[0.3\baselineskip]%
